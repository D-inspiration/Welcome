Winning a competition is exciting.

But sometimes, the most meaningful validation happens before the result is announced.

That was our experience with [TixThreads](/) during the YSA Summit challenge organized within the Port Harcourt North Coordinating Council.

The challenge itself was unusual.

Across the entire council, only six teams could be formed. Participants had less than 36 hours to network, find people they barely knew, build teams of at least five, and develop and present a business idea.

That was how TixThreads entered the challenge.

But something happened during the competition that stood out to us.

## Two judges offered to continue supporting TixThreads

Before we even knew whether we had won, two judges offered us opportunities for continued development and guidance.

One offered an intensive 10-month follow-up and guidance program.

Another offered full development assistance.

For us, that was significant.

A competition win tells you that you performed well against the other teams.

But when experienced judges independently see enough potential in an idea to offer continued support before knowing the final result, that's a different kind of validation.

It suggested that there was something in [TixThreads](/services/tixthreads/) worth taking further.

## The response from tailors has been even more important

TixThreads wasn't created simply because we thought tailors needed another piece of software.

We've been taking the idea directly to tailors and listening to their reactions.

So far, we've spoken with approximately 20 potential customers, and the response has been consistently encouraging.

The tailors we've approached have understood the problem [TixThreads is trying to solve](/services/tixthreads/) and have responded positively to the idea.

That matters because ultimately, TixThreads isn't being built for a competition.

It's being built for real tailoring businesses.

The competition gave us an opportunity to test how other people perceived the idea.

The conversations with tailors are helping us test whether the idea actually solves a problem worth paying attention to.

And the two offers of continued support gave us another signal that we should keep going.

## Winning wasn't the finish line

The competition is now behind us.

The MVP is also here.

But neither of those things is the destination.

The next stage is about taking what we've built and making it genuinely useful for the businesses TixThreads is intended to serve.

We're still learning.

We're still talking to tailors.

We're still improving the product.

And we're still figuring out how far this can go.

But one thing has become increasingly clear:

TixThreads is no longer just an idea.

It's a product that has been built, tested with real potential customers, presented to judges, recognized in competition, and offered further support before we even knew we had won.

Now comes the harder and more exciting part: turning that early validation into a real business.
